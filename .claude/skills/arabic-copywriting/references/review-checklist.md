# Arabic Copy Review Checklist

Use for every Arabic asset before delivery, and when reviewing a client's existing Arabic. Mark each item pass, fix, or n/a. Deliver the diagnosis first, then the fixes.

## A. Fit

- [ ] Register matches the channel, category, and brand (MSA / white Arabic / Saudi dialect / regional dialect).
- [ ] Register is consistent within the asset, or the mix is deliberate and stated per section.
- [ ] Dialect is the audience's own (Najdi/Hejazi/Gulf for Saudi), not Egyptian or Levantine by accident.
- [ ] Gender address is correct for the audience and consistent in every verb, pronoun, and adjective.
- [ ] Tone matches the brand's approved Arabic samples.

## B. Naturalness (translation tells)

- [ ] No English word order; benefit or verb leads.
- [ ] No "نحن" sentence openers by reflex.
- [ ] No filler vocabulary: حلول مبتكرة، تجربة فريدة، نقلة نوعية، رائد، متكامل، سلس، ارتقِ.
- [ ] No "ليس X بل Y" or "لا يتعلق الأمر بـ" contrast reveals.
- [ ] No stacked "بدون X، بدون Y، بدون Z".
- [ ] No "في عالم اليوم" style openers, no "لا تتردد" before CTAs, no "باختصار" closers.
- [ ] No "قم بـ + مصدر" outside formal copy.
- [ ] Idioms are native or absent; no literal English idioms.
- [ ] Reads aloud naturally in the target voice (do the read).
- [ ] Sentence lengths vary; not every line is the same shape.

## C. Correctness

- [ ] Hamza forms correct: إ/أ/ا at the start, ئ/ؤ/ء in the middle and end (إلى، أنت، شيء، مسؤول، سؤال).
- [ ] Taa marbuta vs haa correct (مكتبة not مكتبه; له not لة).
- [ ] Alif maqsura vs yaa correct (على، إلى، متى vs في، التي).
- [ ] Diacritics: only where they disambiguate (وفّر، جرّب، حمّل); none elsewhere unless the brand style asks.
- [ ] Agreement in number and gender between nouns, adjectives, and verbs.
- [ ] Dual and plural forms correct where used.
- [ ] Numbers: Western digits consistently, or Eastern Arabic consistently per brand style; never mixed.
- [ ] Currency and price format consistent (99 ر.س / 99 ريال); VAT-inclusive statement where required.
- [ ] Dates and times in the agreed format; Hijri shown where relevant.
- [ ] Brand and product names follow the glossary (Latin or transliterated, never both in one asset).

## D. Typography and Rendering

- [ ] Arabic punctuation used (، ؟ ؛), no space before punctuation.
- [ ] No em dashes; use commas, full stops, or restructure.
- [ ] Exclamation marks removed.
- [ ] Mixed Arabic/English strings tested in the real layout for bidi order (brand names, numbers with units, URLs, hashtags).
- [ ] Hashtags use underscores, no spaces.
- [ ] Line breaks do not split words; headlines wrap to the intended line count.
- [ ] Font supports all weights used; line height adequate.
- [ ] Length within channel limits (SMS 70 Arabic characters per segment; ad headline limits).

## E. Claims and Compliance

- [ ] Every claim is supportable from the context doc or client evidence; unsupported claims are marked [NEED: ...] with the evidence that would unlock them.
- [ ] Superlatives (الأفضل، الأول، الأرخص) only with proof.
- [ ] Mandatory lines present: VAT, terms, promotion permit number, commercial registration, opt-out, paid-partnership disclosure as applicable.
- [ ] No religious text used commercially; no content that conflicts with Saudi advertising standards.
- [ ] Comparative claims about competitors are factual and defensible.

## F. Parity (bilingual assets)

- [ ] Same facts, prices, legal lines, and CTA intent in Arabic and English.
- [ ] Structure may differ; nothing is missing from either version.
- [ ] Terminology matches the glossary in both languages.
- [ ] Two-column review file provided for approvers.

## Delivery Format for a Review

1. **Diagnosis** (two to four lines): what kind of problem dominates (translated, wrong register, agreement errors, tells, compliance gaps).
2. **Fixed lines**: original, revised, one-line reason. Group by section.
3. **Left as is**: lines that work, so the client knows they were read.
4. **Open items**: [NEED] claims, legal lines to confirm, glossary decisions to make.
