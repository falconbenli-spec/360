## Barrier Analysis

For the behaviour the entity wants, list why the public does not do it now. Classify each barrier:

| Barrier type | Question | Campaign can fix? |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Do they know the service, rule, or option exists? | Yes |
| Understanding | Do they know how, where, and when? | Yes |
| Ability | Can they do it (access, language, documents, cost, time)? | Partly; often a service fix |
| Motivation | Do they see a benefit, or a cost of not acting? | Yes, with honest framing |
| Trust | Do they believe the entity, the data, the privacy of their information? | Slowly; through proof and consistency |
| Social norm | Do they think others like them do it? | Yes, with real norm data |
| Friction | Is the service itself hard? | No; report it to the entity |

Rank the barriers by size with whatever evidence exists (call-center logs, service drop-off data, surveys, social listening). The campaign targets the largest barrier the campaign can actually move. Friction findings go to the entity as a service recommendation.

## Message-to-Action Design

For each target audience:

```
Know:   [the one fact]
Feel:   [the one feeling that motivates, honest and respectful]
Do:     [the one action, with where and how]
Proof:  [why believe it: source, data, authority, peer norm]
```

Rules:
- One action per message. "Register, then update, then share" is three campaigns.
- The action is specific and reachable from the ad: a short link, a QR, an app name, a number.
- Fear-based framing only for safety topics, and always paired with the action and a path to it.
- Positive norms ("أغلب الأسر في [المدينة] سجّلت") only with real data from the entity.
- Deadlines when real; state the date in both calendars where the entity requires.

## Campaign Architecture

| Phase | Purpose | Typical assets |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline | Measure awareness and behaviour before launch | Survey, service data snapshot |
| Launch | Establish the fact and the action | Hero film (30s/15s/6s), key visual, press release, official posts, outdoor |
| Explain | Remove understanding barriers | Explainer videos, step-by-step carousels, FAQ, infographics, sign-language version |
| Motivate | Address motivation and trust | Testimonials from real citizens (with consent), expert voices, proof points |
| Remind | Sustain action over the period | Short reminders, deadline countdowns (real), SMS via the entity |
| Report | Show results and thank the public | Outcome post, SPA release, report |

## Bilingual Messaging Rules

- Arabic master first, in clear formal MSA. Sentence length under about 20 words for public copy. Active voice. Verbs the public uses ("سجّل", "حدّث", "بلّغ").
- English rendering: same meaning, official names, no idioms that do not exist in the Arabic.
- Headlines may differ in wording between languages to read naturally, but not in meaning or claim.
- Numerals: follow the entity's guideline (Arabic-Indic or Western numerals); be consistent across a campaign.
- Dates: Hijri and Gregorian as the entity requires; day names in both languages.
- Avoid loanwords in Arabic where an established Arabic term exists; avoid archaic terms where a plain one exists.

## Channel Roles for Public Campaigns

| Channel | Role | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| SPA and national TV/press | Authority and reach | Official announcement; interviews with officials; approved quotes |
| Official X account | Primary public channel | Announcement, explainers, replies to service questions |
| Instagram, Snapchat, TikTok | Reach younger and family audiences | Vertical explainers; creators only with entity approval and strict briefs |
| YouTube | Hosting films; pre-roll | Long explainers with chapters |
| Search | Capture intent | Service name, common questions, misspellings |
| Outdoor and in-venue | Reminder at the point of action | Service centers, airports, roads, malls |
| SMS and app push (entity-sent) | Direct reminder | Agency drafts; entity sends through its own licensed channels |
| Radio | Repetition for behaviour campaigns | Drive-time spots; regional stations |
| Field and partners | Trusted intermediaries | Schools, universities, chambers, mosques via the relevant authority |

## Measurement Plan

| Objective | Indicator | Source | Baseline | Target | Timing |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Aided/unaided recall | Survey (pre/post) | | | Pre, post |
| Understanding | Correct answers to 3 comprehension questions | Survey; call-center question mix | | | Pre, post |
| Adoption | Registrations / applications / usage | Entity service data | | | Weekly |
| Behaviour | Compliance or incident rate | Entity operational data | | | Monthly, seasonally adjusted |
| Sentiment | Share of positive/neutral/negative mentions | Social listening | | | Weekly |
| Delivery | Reach, frequency, views, clicks | Platforms | | | Weekly (inputs, not outcomes) |

State the methodology for surveys (sample, method, dates) and for listening (tools, keywords, languages). Report inputs separately from outcomes so reach is never presented as impact.

## Examples of Framing (illustrative, adapt to the entity's voice)

Awareness of a new digital service:
```
الخدمة صارت أقرب. أنجز [الخدمة] من جوالك عبر [التطبيق] خلال دقائق.
```
Behaviour change with a safety frame and an action:
```
حزام الأمان يحميك أنت ومن معك. اربطه قبل التحرك، في كل رحلة.
```
Participation with a real norm:
```
أكثر من [رقم موثق] مشارك سجّلوا حتى الآن. سجّل قبل [التاريخ] عبر [الرابط].
```
Each example needs the entity's approved facts before use; replace every bracket with sourced content.
